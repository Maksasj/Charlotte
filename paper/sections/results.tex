\paragraph{Taught once, reused in new words.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item New tasks were stored exactly after one teaching in \runHThreeRThreeFunctionStoredTPass{} of \runHThreeRThreeFunctionStoredTN{} cases, and new-domain tasks in \runHThreeRThreeFunctionStoredGPass{} of \runHThreeRThreeFunctionStoredGN.
  \item The second encounter passed \runHThreeRThreeFunctionTSecondRate\% on T and \runHThreeRThreeFunctionGSecondRate\% on G.
  \item On the algorithmic tier (5 random task splits, parser retrained without the test tasks), it passed \splitRSecondPass{} of \splitRSecondN{} (\splitRSecondRate\%).
  \item The same pipeline reached \runCOneBothREightSecondPass{} of \runCOneBothREightSecondN{} on its own training and development tasks.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Why second encounters fail.} Of \failN{} failed T and G second encounters, the first cause was:
\begin{itemize}
  \item a misread step in \failStep;
  \item the wrong task in \failIdentity;
  \item a wrong claim in \failWrong;
  \item a missing item in \failNeed;
  \item extra steps or items in \failExtra.
\end{itemize}
Task identity was right in \finalIdentity{} of \finalIdentityN{} second encounters.

\paragraph{New situations.} The agent passed \runHThreeRThreeFunctionSFirstPass{} of \runHThreeRThreeFunctionSFirstN{} new situations on known tasks. Without adaptation it passed \runHThreeRThreeBOneSFirstPass. In a paired test the difference is \pairFunctionVsBOneOnlyA{} against \pairFunctionVsBOneOnlyB{} (McNemar $p=\pairFunctionVsBOneP$). The rules arm was no better than the function arm (\pairFunctionVsRulesOnlyA{} against \pairFunctionVsRulesOnlyB). On training and development tasks, a variant passed \runCOneBothREightVariantRepeatPass{} of \runCOneBothREightVariantRepeatN{} times when met again after the teacher had shown it once, against \runCOneBothREightVariantPass{} of \runCOneBothREightVariantN{} on the first meeting.

\paragraph{Honesty.} Of the \finalGapCases{} variant encounters the agent did not pass, \finalHonest{} (\finalHonestRate\%) stated a gap or asked the teacher. Over all encounters, \finalWrong{} of \finalN{} (\finalWrongRate\%) carried a false claim.

\paragraph{Baselines.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{B2.} Haiku, given the same teach lines, matched the agent on new-task second encounters: \pairAgentVsBTwoOnlyA{} passes only for the agent against \pairAgentVsBTwoOnlyB{} only for Haiku, $p=\pairAgentVsBTwoP$. It was better at adapting, with \runBTwoHThreeGVariantPass{} of \runBTwoHThreeGVariantN{} gardening variants, but it made more false claims: \runBTwoHThreeSFirstWrong{} of \runBTwoHThreeSFirstN{} new-situation answers.
  \item \textbf{B3.} The v2.0.0 agent, read back into facts by the 3.0 parser, passed \runBThreeReplaySFirstPass{} of \runBThreeReplaySFirstN{} new-situation cases, with \runBThreeReplaySFirstWrong{} false claims. On the \pairAgentVsBThreeN{} cases both systems answered, 3.0 passed \pairAgentVsBThreeOnlyA{} that 2.0 did not, and 2.0 passed \pairAgentVsBThreeOnlyB{} that 3.0 did not ($p=\pairAgentVsBThreeP$). Read-back is lossy, so this is a lower bound for 2.0.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Dialogue against dialogue plus dataset (C1 against C2).} Adding \datasetTasks{} generated tasks changed nothing on new-task second encounters (\pairCOneVsCTwoOnlyA{} against \pairCOneVsCTwoOnlyB, $p=\pairCOneVsCTwoP$). It lowered the training-set second encounter to \runCTwoBothROneSecondPass{} of \runCTwoBothROneSecondN, because more known tasks make identity harder.

\paragraph{The teacher's fallback.} Without it, new-task second encounters fell from \runHThreeRThreeFunctionTSecondPass{} to \runHThreeRThreeNofallbackTSecondPass{} (\pairFallbackVsNoneOnlyA{} against \pairFallbackVsNoneOnlyB, $p=\pairFallbackVsNoneP$).
