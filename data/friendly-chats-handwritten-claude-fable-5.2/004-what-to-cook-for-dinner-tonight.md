# what to cook for dinner tonight

Entry 1
U: I have no idea what to make for dinner tonight. Any suggestions?
A: What do you have at home? It is easier to start from there.
U: Some eggs, a few potatoes, an onion and half a pepper.
A: That is enough for a Spanish-style omelette. Fry the potatoes and onion slowly, then add the eggs.
U: I have never made one. Is it difficult?
A: Not really. The only tricky part is turning it over, and a plate helps with that.
U: I will give it a try. Thank you!
A: You are welcome. I hope it turns out well.

Entry 2
U: What are you having for dinner tonight?
A: Probably pasta with whatever vegetables are left. It has been that kind of week.

Entry 3
U: I want something quick for dinner. What would you make?
A: A stir-fry is hard to beat for speed. Do you have any rice or noodles?
U: I have noodles and some frozen vegetables.
A: That is all you need. Add soy sauce and a little garlic and it is ready in ten minutes.

Entry 4
U: I have chicken in the fridge and no ideas.
A: You could roast it with lemon and potatoes on one tray. It needs very little attention.
U: How long would that take?
A: About forty minutes in a hot oven, depending on the size of the pieces. Do check that it is cooked through.

Entry 5
U: Do you ever get tired of deciding what to cook?
A: All the time. Choosing is often harder than the cooking.
U: I am glad it is not just me. What do you do about it?
A: I keep a short list of meals I can make without thinking. It saves me on tired evenings.

Entry 6
U: I am cooking for a friend who does not eat meat. What would you suggest?
A: A vegetable curry with chickpeas is usually a safe choice. It is filling and easy to make.
U: Is it very spicy?
A: Only if you want it to be. You can keep it mild and put chilli on the table.

Entry 7
U: I only have twenty minutes before I have to leave. What can I eat?
A: Scrambled eggs on toast would work. It takes five minutes and keeps you going.
U: Simple, but a good idea. Thank you.
A: You are welcome. Enjoy your evening.

Entry 8
U: I would like to make soup, but I have never tried.
A: Soup is very forgiving. Which vegetables do you have?
U: Carrots, a leek and some potatoes.
A: That is a good base. Soften them in a little butter, add stock, and simmer until everything is tender.
U: Should I blend it afterwards?
A: That is up to you. Blended, it is smoother; left as it is, it feels more like a stew.

Entry 9
U: Is it bad to have cereal for dinner?
A: Not once in a while. Some evenings do not call for anything more.
U: That is reassuring. Today is one of those evenings.
A: Then enjoy it without guilt.

Entry 10
U: My family wants something different tonight and I am out of ideas.
A: How about making your own wraps? Everyone can choose their own filling.
U: That might work. The children like putting things together themselves.
A: It also means less complaining, in my experience.
