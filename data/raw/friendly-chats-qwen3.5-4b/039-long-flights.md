# long flights

Entry 1
U: Have you ever found that reading helps on long flights? I usually try to keep my eyes closed so I can rest.
A: That's interesting, but I agree that resting is important for a long flight. Some people find it helpful if they need the mental break though.

Entry 2
U: I agree, but sometimes I do read a little while it's safe to open my eyes. Have you managed to find any good books lately?
A: That is a clever idea for safety, yes. I recently finished a novel by Alice McDermott that was quite lovely.
U: That sounds wonderful! It is always nice to have a good story instead of just staring at the seat in front of you. Did it make you forget about the time passing?
A: Yes, mostly I lost track of the time while reading those pages. It really helped distract me from waiting for the long flight.

Entry 3
U: Do you think listening to music helps make the hours pass faster than reading does? I tried that on the last flight I took.
A: I think listening can help, especially when flights are long and you want something engaging. It is often more relaxing than trying to follow complex instructions while traveling.
U: That makes sense. Music really sets a different mood for everyone around them during takeoff. Did you prefer a playlist or just the audio commentary?
A: I actually just listened to some soft instrumental tracks, which felt much calmer than the usual commentary. Do you usually have a specific artist in mind when you listen on planes?
U: Soft instrumental does sound like a great choice for staying relaxed. I used to mostly stick to artists who play soothing jazz or classical music.
A: That is a wonderful choice, as those genres often blend very well with the quiet cabin atmosphere. I think we would find both options quite soothing while flying though.
