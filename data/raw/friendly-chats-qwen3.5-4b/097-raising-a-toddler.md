# raising a toddler

Entry 1
U: Has anyone found any good tricks for getting toddlers to eat their vegetables? I find myself staring at empty plates quite often.
A: That is really frustrating when you work so hard on preparing food. It seems like many parents struggle with the same thing, doesn't it?
U: I guess most of us do, but sometimes I wonder if we are just being too strict about it. Do you ever try hiding veggies inside other things like sandwiches or pasta?
A: That is a clever idea, and many people do use that strategy successfully. It does take some creativity to get the flavors right though. Have you tried mixing chopped veggies into sauces yet?

Entry 2
U: I've tried hiding veggies in muffins but it seems like the toddler is pretty picky about textures these days. Have you discovered anything else that works well?
A: That's a tough spot when they are so sensitive to texture. Maybe trying different forms like shreds or purees could be more appealing to them.
U: I'll definitely try those ideas next time, especially the purees since they taste better anyway. Have you found anything that actually makes them sit down for a meal?
A: That is a great angle, as most foods are sweeter when blended smooth. My own toddler just likes everything very soft or mushy for now.

Entry 3
U: I have been struggling with morning routines lately, so it feels like a big task when they refuse to eat. Have you found any good tricks for getting toddlers to eat their vegetables?
A: That is such a tough part of parenting, and I hope your little ones start eating soon. Has anyone recommended specific food hiding methods that worked well for you?
U: I actually tried mixing spinach into pizza sauce recently, and they seem to have gone down it happily. It was worth the effort to make!
A: That is a clever trick since most toddlers love pizza toppings. Have you also tried baking them with their favorite meals?
U: Yes, I sometimes bake mashed bananas or applesauce into muffins for breakfast. It does change up the texture enough that they usually eat it without complaint.
A: That sounds like a really nice idea to keep things interesting in the morning. Do you think mixing in different flavors like cinnamon works better than plain fruit?
U: I actually just added a little bit of cinnamon to some muffins and it was very popular. I think adding flavor helps make them seem more appealing to picky eaters.
A: That makes total sense, flavor is often key with picky eaters. Have you found other small additions like nut butter or cheese that they enjoy too?
U: Nut butter might work well since some toddlers love the texture, but cheese can be tricky if they are really picky about certain foods. It definitely helps to have a little bit of something new in there though.
A: That is a great point about texture being important for them. Have you tried letting them help make the muffins so they feel more involved with the meal?
U: I have let them stir the batter, and it really does make them take a bigger bite to see what I did. It seems like the involvement is actually quite helpful.
A: That is wonderful; involving kids often makes them much more enthusiastic about trying new foods. Have you noticed if they are less likely to reject the veggies once they helped mix it in?
