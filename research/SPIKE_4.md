## 9. Findings for phase B

1. **The inducer is too permissive.** At k = 3 it keeps 68 hypotheses. 33 pairs of them support each other (A from B and B from A), and layers reach 10. Support counts inherited subjects of one kind as separate evidence, so a single taught fact about a kind can supply all the support. Learned rules add 1 correct answers but 2 wrong ones, which is the failed gate condition. Phase B needs three changes: support counted over taught subjects only (or one per kind), no rule whose support depends on a rule it supports, and acceptance only for rules that predict something outside their support's kinds.
2. **Naming drift.** The detector asked 16 questions and learned 12 aliases. The score was the same with it on and off (70 against 70 correct). The rewritten symbols rarely sat on a question's proof path in this fixture, so the drift test does not separate the settings yet.
3. **Scaling.** At x50 (34598 taught facts) full closure takes 1378 ms and every answer matches x1. Answering recomputes the model for each question's `given` facts (154937 ms for 100 questions). The per-question demand slice (design 7.5) is needed before size L.
4. **Rules that never mattered here.** B3, B5 and B9 changed no answer: the fixture's conflicts are all answered by the oracle, its part chains are short, and gaps are scored as the absence of a claim. They are kept as safety rules; their unique contribution is 0 on this fixture.
