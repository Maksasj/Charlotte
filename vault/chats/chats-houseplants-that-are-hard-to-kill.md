Entry 1
U: I keep forgetting to water my plants. Is there anything that can survive me?
A: A snake plant would be a good choice. It copes well with little water and not much light.
U: That sounds like my kind of plant. How often does it need watering?
A: Every two or three weeks is usually enough. It is more likely to suffer from too much water than too little.

Entry 2
U: Which plant would you give to a complete beginner?
A: A pothos, probably. It grows in most rooms and tells you when it is thirsty by drooping.

Entry 3
U: My flat gets almost no sunlight. Can I still have plants?
A: Yes, a few do well in low light. A ZZ plant or a snake plant should manage.
U: Do they need special care?
A: Very little. Water them sparingly and they are usually content.

Entry 4
U: I think I killed my cactus. How is that even possible?
A: It happens more often than you would think. Usually the cause is too much water.
U: I watered it every week.
A: That is probably it. Most cacti need far less, especially in winter.

Entry 5
U: The leaves on my plant are turning yellow. What does that mean?
A: It can mean several things, but too much water is the most common cause. Is the soil wet?
U: Yes, it is quite soggy.
A: Then let it dry out before you water again. Make sure the pot can drain as well.

Entry 6
U: I am away for three weeks. Will my plants survive?
A: Some will. Succulents and snake plants should be fine for that long.
U: And the others?
A: You could ask a neighbour, or move them out of direct sun so they dry more slowly.

Entry 7
U: Do plants really make a room feel better?
A: I think they do. A bit of green makes a room feel more lived in.
U: I have one small fern and I am quite fond of it.
A: Ferns are lovely. They like a little more moisture than most, so keep an eye on it.

Entry 8
U: Is a spider plant easy to look after?
A: Yes, it is one of the easiest. It also produces small offshoots that you can pot up.
U: So I could end up with several plants from one?
A: You could. People often give the little ones away to friends.

Entry 9
U: I bought a plant and I do not know what it is.
A: What does it look like?
U: It has thick, shiny leaves and it is about knee height.
A: It could be a rubber plant, but I am not certain from that alone. Showing a photo at a garden shop would settle it.

Entry 10
U: How do I know when to repot a plant?
A: A good sign is roots coming out of the bottom of the pot. Slow growth can be another.
U: Mine has roots circling the surface.
A: Then it is probably time. Choose a pot only a little larger than the old one.
